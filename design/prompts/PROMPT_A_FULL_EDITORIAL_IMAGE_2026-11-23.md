## A. PROMPT UTAMA — FULL EDITORIAL IMAGE (ONE SHOT)

> **Salin seluruh isi bagian A ini ke percakapan ChatGPT Image Generation.** Gunakan hanya sebagai kandidat visual; hasilnya wajib diperiksa dan diperbaiki. Jika model tidak dapat mempertahankan tulisan kecil dengan benar, gunakan **Prompt B** dan tambahkan teks melalui program.

Create **one original, publication-quality, square (1:1) global news infographic** titled **“THE WORLD IN ONE DAY”**, summarizing the world's most consequential events **on or first reported Sunday, 22 November 2026**.

This is a premium international newspaper front page combined with a world-news editorial atlas, **not a social media collage**, not a dashboard UI, not an imitation of any existing competitor image. Aim for a compelling at-a-glance hierarchy and excellent readability when the square image is viewed at approximately **300 pixels wide**. Prioritize **correct dated events, global significance, truthfulness, and legibility above decorative detail**.

### Art direction — locked

- Format: perfectly square, intended final export **1024 × 1024 px**.
- Art style: original **flat-vector editorial illustrations**, geometric silhouettes, precise shapes, restrained shading, sharp edges, elegant newspaper illustration language; no stock photos, no photorealistic tragedy, no copied art.
- Palette: warm ivory `#F7F3E9` paper; deep ink navy `#14213C`; restrained editorial red `#A9322D`; teal `#23767A`; ochre `#C89034`; muted green `#497A56`.
- Typography: bold editorial serif for the date and main headlines, strong clean sans-serif for region labels, small labels, and status; no more than two font families; excellent kerning and alignment.
- Layout: consistent grid, 16px outer margins, ample internal padding, strong contrast, thin dividers, no unnecessary gradients, glassmorphism, shadows, fake logos, watermarks, or random decorations.
- **Do not reproduce or trace the visual composition of a specific make.fun competitor.** Build a new original page with editorial sophistication.

### Information hierarchy — locked

1. **Large masthead / date (top 85px):** `THE WORLD IN ONE DAY` with a dominant, unmistakable date: `SUNDAY, 22 NOVEMBER 2026`.
2. **Short editorial deckline (next ~28px):** `CLIMATE DEADLOCK · SECURITY SHOCKS · POLITICAL SHIFT` — only if the text fits cleanly.
3. **Key numbers (next ~42px):** three concise, well-attributed data points, for example: `258` US House Democratic seats called Sunday; `186` drones reported by Ukraine for the night of 21–22 November; `24` body bags recovered in Indonesia **(never label 24 as confirmed deaths)**. If attribution cannot be readable, omit the disputed number rather than make an unqualified claim.
4. **Thin region/category ribbon (~22px):** `GLOBAL · AFRICA · EUROPE · AMERICAS · MIDDLE EAST · ASIA` — geographic breadth without made-up story counts.
5. **Hero zone (~266px): THREE main stories**. Make the COP31 story visibly dominant (~46% of hero width), with Libya and USA each taking approximately one quarter.
6. **Secondary zone (~238px): SEVEN story cards** with a small distinct illustration, exact headline, place, and restrained status. One optional space can be used for a tiny world map only if the map adds clarity; never distort geography to fill space.
7. **Brief zone (~188px): EIGHT one-line news briefs**, arranged in two neat columns. If the writing becomes cramped or unreadable, **reduce to 6–7 briefs** instead of shrinking the type or creating broken text.
8. **Footer (~54px):** compact `DATE CHECK`, source-family line, and status legend `REPORTED · CLAIM · CONFIRMED`. Do not list fabricated source URLs or fake detailed citations.

### Three lead stories — exact editorial meaning

Illustrate an international plenary hall, a COP31 gavel beside an **unadopted** cover-decision document and negotiating delegations. Avoid portraying every COP31 decision as a failure; **do not draw an approved climate treaty**. Status: `CONFIRMED`.

**LEAD 2 — AFRICA / SECURITY / BENGHAZI, LIBYA**  
Headline: `RUSSIAN WARSHIP CAPSIZES IN LIBYA`  
Small explanatory line: `Russian corvette Merkury overturned after a suspected drone attack.`  
Illustrate the port of Benghazi with a damaged, capsized naval corvette as a **symbolic editorial scene**, not a claimed documentary photograph. **Never show a Ukrainian flag or identify an unproven attacker.** Status: `REPORTED`.

**LEAD 3 — AMERICAS / POLITICS / UNITED STATES**  
Headline: `US DEMOCRATS REACH 258 SEATS`  
Small explanatory line: `House majority reaches at least 258 after a Sunday race call.`  
Illustrate the U.S. Capitol, an accurate stylized House seat indicator, and a clear large **258**. Do **not** show polling booths as if the election itself happened on November 22; it took place earlier. Status: `REPORTED`.

Use one compact card per news story, with a **distinct and locally appropriate original icon/scene**. Keep these subordinate to the three lead stories:

1. `UKRAINE · EUROPE` — **`UKRAINE REPORTS 186 DRONES`** — night of November 21–22; attribution to Ukrainian Air Force. Scene: air-defense silhouettes over a city; do not depict all drones as striking buildings. Status `REPORTED`.

### World briefs — exact wording, subject to date check

Place short, unambiguous lines in a structured **“ALSO ON SUNDAY”** section. These are lower priority than the ten story cards and may be omitted for legibility or if the final fact-check rejects them:

- `WEST BANK — Arson attacks reported`.
- `POLAND — Investigators identify missile debris` **(investigation update on Sunday, not new missile incursion)**.
- `THAILAND — Kok River pollution proposal` **(proposal, not enacted policy)**.
- `US–NVIDIA — Washington seeks chip-sale answers` **(inquiry, not proven wrongdoing)**.
- `INDIA — Water-quality regulator plans action` **(limited samples, not blanket product recall)**.
- `BRAZIL — Storm death toll revised Sunday` **(storm itself happened Friday)**.
- `BAHRAIN — Sunday injury update after attack` **(attack was Saturday local)**.
- `SEATTLE — Airport detention dispute` **(event Saturday local, Sunday reporting in other zones; OMIT if its dated eligibility is doubtful)**.

**Never invent a ninth brief, extra breaking news, country, statistic, casualty number, logo, flag, timestamp, map pin, or headline.** If any brief is unclear, omit it and preserve negative space.

### Data ethics and editorial precision

- Distinguish **confirmed occurrence** from **one side's claim**. Never present unverified targets or casualty estimates as established fact.
- On COP31 do not write `DEAL APPROVED`, `COP31 SUCCEEDED`, or `COP31 FAILED COMPLETELY`.
- On Libya, do not attribute responsibility to Ukraine or any other party as a fact.
- On Oman, do not replace an incident report with imagery of verified mines or a sinking tanker.
- On Indonesia, do not equate **24 body bags** with **24 confirmed fatalities**.
- On Ukraine, do not mix the `148` drones from a different overnight period with the `186` reported for 21–22 November.
- Avoid local-21-November events unless an actual Sunday update or permissible first-report timezone is clearly identified.
- Any illustration is symbolic editorial art; it should not pretend to be a real wire photograph.

### Composition quality controls

Make the date and COP31 headline readable instantly; make the Libya and USA leads visually distinct within a few seconds; leave meaningful whitespace around every card. Use a coherent illustrated world-news language. Keep **no more than 3 numerical callouts**, and do not add a timeline if accurate times are not provided. Use a mini-map only if geopolitical locations can be represented correctly and the labels still fit.

**Typography is the critical failure mode.** Render every exact English title and number correctly, without pseudo-letters, gibberish, typos, clipped text, overlap, or unreadably small footnotes. If the model cannot render all briefs accurately, **remove lower-priority briefs**; never hallucinate replacement text.

Output **one finished square editorial image** (no mockup frame, browser chrome, explanatory caption outside the square, or multiple variants). It should feel like a bespoke premium front page that is simultaneously informative, credible, modern, and visually exceptional.
